How do stable, shareable classical records emerge from continuous unitary evolution?

2.1 Open quantum systems and the partial trace

2.1.1 Why subsystems lose purity under entanglement

Real sensors, brains, and computers are never isolated. Any subsystem \(S\) interacts continuously with a vast environment \(E\) (photons, air molecules, phonons, thermal ions) inside a joint Hilbert space \(\mathcal{H}_S \otimes \mathcal{H}_E\). Suppose \(S\) begins in a pure superposition \(\alpha\ket{0} + \beta\ket{1}\) and couples to an environment initialized in \(\ket{E_0}\):

\[(\alpha\ket{0} + \beta\ket{1})\ket{E_0} \xrightarrow{U_{SE}} \alpha\ket{0}\ket{E_0'} + \beta\ket{1}\ket{E_1'}.\]

To predict any local measurement on \(S\) alone, we take the partial trace over \(E\):

\[\rho_S = \operatorname{Tr}_E\bigl(\ketbra{\Psi_{SE}}{\Psi_{SE}}\bigr) = |\alpha|^2\ketbra{0}{0} + |\beta|^2\ketbra{1}{1} + \alpha\beta^*\braket{E_1'|E_0'}\ketbra{0}{1} + \alpha^*\beta\braket{E_0'|E_1'}\ketbra{1}{0}.\]

The off-diagonal terms—which encode quantum interference between \(\ket{0}\) and \(\ket{1}\)—are multiplied by the environmental overlap \(\braket{E_1'|E_0'}\). As \(S\) scatters even a modest number of environmental particles, \(\braket{E_1'|E_0'} \to 0\) exponentially fast, leaving

\[\rho_S \approx |\alpha|^2\ketbra{0}{0} + |\beta|^2\ketbra{1}{1}.\]

Notice that no collapse postulate was added. Unitary evolution simply delocalized phase information into correlations across \(S\) and \(E\), rendering it inaccessible to local observers.

Worked check 2.1. If \(S\) couples identically to \(N\) independent environment qubits such that each qubit has overlap \(|\braket{e_1|e_0}| = \gamma < 1\), how does the off-diagonal coherence scale with \(N\)?

Reasoned solution

The joint overlap is the product \(\braket{E_1'|E_0'} = \prod_{k=1}^N \braket{e_1|e_0}\), whose magnitude is \(\gamma^N = e^{-N|\ln\gamma|}\). Even for \(\gamma = 0.99\), \(N = 1000\) reduces coherence to \(\sim 4\times 10^{-5}\).

2.2 Pointer states and einselection

2.2.1 Stability against environmental monitoring

Why do we see a chair at a definite location rather than in a superposition of two locations? Decoherence does not treat all bases equally. Suppose the system–environment interaction Hamiltonian takes the form \(H_{\text{int}} = \sum_k S_k \otimes E_k\). States \(\ket{s_i}\) that are simultaneous eigenstates of the system coupling operators \(S_k\) satisfy

\[H_{\text{int}}\ket{s_i}\ket{E_0} = \ket{s_i} \otimes \Bigl(\sum_k s_{k,i} E_k\ket{E_0}\Bigr).\]

Such states do not get entangled with \(E\) by \(H_{\text{int}}\); they only accumulate phases on the environment. Zurek called these robust states pointer states, and the process by which the environment continuously selects them environment-induced superselection (einselection). Superpositions of distinct pointer states rapidly decohere into classical-looking mixtures, whereas pointer states themselves persist long enough to serve as reliable physical records.

For macroscopic objects, interactions depend strongly on spatial position \(x\) (via Coulomb forces and photon scattering), so localized wavepackets in position and momentum emerge as pointer states rather than energy eigenstates or Schrödinger-cat superpositions.

Worked check 2.2. Suppose a qubit couples to a bath via \(H_{\text{int}} = g Z \otimes E\). Which single-qubit states are pointer states, and what happens to \(\ket{+}\)?

Reasoned solution

Since \([Z, \ketbra{0}{0}] = [Z, \ketbra{1}{1}] = 0\), the computational basis states \(\ket{0}\) and \(\ket{1}\) are the pointer states. The state \(\ket{+} = (\ket{0}+\ket{1})/\sqrt{2}\) decoheres rapidly into the diagonal mixture \(I/2\).

2.3 Quantum Darwinism and objective records

2.3.1 Redundant imprints across fragments of the environment

Einselection explains why pointer states survive locally, yet observers never measure the whole environment—we sample tiny fragments of it, such as a sliver of photons entering our eyes. Why do many independent observers agree on the same classical outcome?

Quantum Darwinism answers this through redundant broadcasting. When a system in pointer basis \(\{\ket{i}\}\) interacts with an environment composed of \(N\) sub-environments \(E_1, \dots, E_N\), a branching state forms:

\[\ket{\Psi_{SE}} = \sum_i c_i \ket{i}_S \otimes \ket{i}_{E_1} \otimes \ket{i}_{E_2} \otimes \cdots \otimes \ket{i}_{E_N}.\]

Even a small fragment \(F \subset E\) containing just a handful of sub-environments shares nearly the full classical mutual information \(I(S:F) \approx H(S)\) with the pointer observable of \(S\). Meanwhile, quantum phase information remains locked in global correlations requiring nearly all of \(E\) to access. Thus, the environment acts as a broadcasting medium: it hides quantum coherence globally while amplifying classical pointer facts locally into thousands of redundant copies.

This gives us our first firm pillar: objective physical records exist purely as a consequence of open-system unitary dynamics, before any intelligent agent or conscious mind enters the scene.

Worked check 2.3. In a GHZ-like branching state \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{0}_S\ket{0}^{\otimes N} + \ket{1}_S\ket{1}^{\otimes N})\), how much mutual information does a single environment qubit \(E_1\) share with \(S\)?

Reasoned solution

Tracing out \(E_2\dots E_N\) gives \(\rho_{SE_1} = \frac{1}{2}\ketbra{00}{00} + \frac{1}{2}\ketbra{11}{11}\). Its entropy is \(S(\rho_{SE_1}) = 1\) bit, while \(S(\rho_S) = 1\) and \(S(\rho_{E_1}) = 1\). Thus \(I(S:E_1) = 1 + 1 - 1 = 1\) bit—every single environment qubit holds a full classical copy of the \(Z\)-basis record.
