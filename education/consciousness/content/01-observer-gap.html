Why does quantum mechanics invoke observers and measurements without ever defining what an observer is?

1.1 The missing observer in classical and quantum mechanics

1.1.1 From passive spectators to interactive measurements

In classical mechanics, an observer is an invisible ghost. A planet orbits the Sun according to Newton’s laws whether anyone watches it or not; reading its position simply reveals a pre-existing coordinate \((x(t), p(t))\) with arbitrarily small disturbance. Because observation can be idealized as passive, classical physics never needed a physical definition of the observer, let alone of intelligence or consciousness.

Quantum mechanics breaks that symmetry. Its textbook rules divide the world into two parts: a quantum system evolving deterministically under a unitary operator \(U = e^{-iHt/\hbar}\), and an external measuring agent who chooses a basis, applies a projector or positive operator-valued measure (POVM), and records a single outcome with Born probability \(p(k) = \operatorname{Tr}(\rho E_k)\). Yet the formalism never specifies which collections of atoms qualify as an apparatus or observer.

To see the gap sharply, model a detector as a second quantum system with ready state \(\ket{A_r}\) and distinguishable record states \(\ket{A_0}, \ket{A_1}\). An ideal measurement interaction acts linearly as

\[U\ket{0}\ket{A_r} = \ket{0}\ket{A_0}, \qquad U\ket{1}\ket{A_r} = \ket{1}\ket{A_1}.\]

If the measured qubit starts in \(\alpha\ket{0} + \beta\ket{1}\), linearity forces the closed joint system into an entangled superposition:

\[U(\alpha\ket{0} + \beta\ket{1})\ket{A_r} = \alpha\ket{0,A_0} + \beta\ket{1,A_1}.\]

The equation produces correlation, not a single classical outcome. Somewhere between microscopic amplitudes and a conscious experimenter reading a screen, physics invokes an “observer”—without saying what physical organization turns a collection of particles into one.

Worked check 1.1. Suppose a qubit starts in \(\ket{+} = (\ket{0}+\ket{1})/\sqrt{2}\) and interacts with an apparatus via the unitary \(U\) above. What is the purity \(\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_A^2)\) of the apparatus alone?

Reasoned solution

The joint state is \((\ket{0,A_0}+\ket{1,A_1})/\sqrt{2}\). Tracing out the qubit gives \(\rho_A = \frac{1}{2}\ketbra{A_0}{A_0} + \frac{1}{2}\ketbra{A_1}{A_1}\), so \(\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_A^2) = (1/2)^2 + (1/2)^2 = 1/2 < 1\). Locally, the apparatus looks like a classical coin flip, even though the joint state remains pure.

1.2 Why naive quantum mysticism fails

1.2.1 Unitarity, no-signalling, and empirical consistency

Early pioneers such as von Neumann and Wigner noted that one could push the boundary between quantum system and classical observer all the way to the brain—and asked whether consciousness itself might trigger wavefunction collapse. That speculation, often caricatured in popular writing as “mind creates reality,” runs into three severe physical obstacles.

First, arbitrariness and circularity: saying “consciousness collapses the wavefunction” explains neither consciousness nor collapse unless we already have a physical criterion for which systems are conscious. Did the early universe wait billions of years for biological brains before forming stars and galaxies? Geological and cosmological records say no.

Second, no-signalling: suppose Alice and Bob share a maximally entangled Bell state \(\ket{\Phi^+} = (\ket{00}+\ket{11})/\sqrt{2}\). Regardless of what local unitary gate, measurement, or mental act Alice performs on her qubit, Bob’s reduced density matrix remains

\[\rho_B = \operatorname{Tr}_A(\ketbra{\Phi^+}{\Phi^+}) = \frac{I}{2}.\]

Any theory claiming that conscious intention biases remote measurement statistics violates the no-signalling theorem and relativistic causality.

Third, experimental precision: macroscopic interference and quantum-optics experiments test unitary evolution and decoherence rates across ten or more orders of magnitude. Any non-unitary modification tied to mind must respect strict empirical bounds on spontaneous heating, phase noise, and Born-rule frequencies.

Worked check 1.2. Could an observer’s conscious choice of measurement basis on qubit \(A\) of \(\ket{\Phi^+}\) change the probability of Bob obtaining \(\ket{0}\) on qubit \(B\)?

Reasoned solution

No. For any measurement basis \(\{\ket{u_0}, \ket{u_1}\}\) chosen by Alice, summing conditional probabilities weighted by Alice’s outcome probabilities always returns \(\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_B \ketbra{0}{0}) = 1/2\).

1.3 Ground rules for a physical theory of mind and agency

1.3.1 Separating operational records, intelligence, and consciousness

To avoid confusion, this book enforces a strict separation among three phenomena that are routinely conflated:

  • Physical records: stable correlations imprinted in a subsystem and its environment (for instance, a silver-halide grain or a magnetic domain). Records require decoherence and thermodynamics, not minds.
  • Intelligence: a functional, third-person property of an open physical system that senses its environment, maintains an internal predictive model, and selects actions to achieve goals under energy and memory constraints.
  • Consciousness: a structural, first-person property—unified, intrinsic experience arising when an autonomous physical subsystem maintains an irreducible, self-referential model of its own state within its environment.

Our objective is to define both intelligence (Chapter 4) and consciousness (Chapter 9) in terms that are completely compatible with standard quantum mechanics. Throughout the course, we will demand four ground rules: every definition must respect (1) unitary evolution of closed systems, (2) open-system decoherence and thermodynamics, (3) relativistic no-signalling, and (4) clear operational criteria distinguishing conscious systems, non-conscious intelligent systems, and mere physical matter.

Worked check 1.3. Why is it helpful to define physical records before defining intelligence, and intelligence before consciousness?

Reasoned solution

Intelligence requires storing and updating beliefs about an environment, which presupposes stable physical records. Consciousness, in turn, builds on integrated, self-referential modeling rather than passive storage, so separating the layers prevents circular definitions.
