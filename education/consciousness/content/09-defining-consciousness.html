How do we define consciousness rigorously without breaking unitarity, decoherence, or relativistic locality?

9.1 Three necessary pillars: integration, autonomy, self-modeling

9.1.1 Why each pillar eliminates trivial counterexamples

Every naive definition of consciousness fails on simple physical counterexamples. Defining consciousness merely as “quantum entanglement” counts a two-qubit Bell pair \(\ket{\Phi^+}\) as conscious. Defining it merely as “information processing” counts a pocket calculator. Defining it merely as “feedback control” counts a wall thermostat.

To exclude trivial artifacts while remaining strictly inside quantum physics, a conscious subsystem \(C \subset \mathcal{H}_{\text{total}}\) must satisfy three joint conditions:

  1. Dynamical Integration (\(\Phi > \Phi_{\min}\)): Across every bipartition \(C_1 \mid C_2\) of \(C\)’s coarse-grained computational subspace, the effective open-system channel \(\mathcal{E}_C\) cannot be decomposed into independent parts. This solves the binding problem: \(C\) acts as one irreducible whole rather than parallel independent modules.
  2. Thermodynamic Autonomy: \(C\) maintains a persistent low-entropy internal boundary against environmental noise and thermal equilibrium by exporting entropy (\(\Delta Q_E \ge k_B T \Delta S\)) over timescales far exceeding microscopic decoherence \(\tau_D\).
  3. Reflexive Self-World Modeling: \(C\)’s internal pointer dynamics encode a compressed predictive model not only of external sensory streams \(E\), but also of \(C\)’s own internal states, attention, and boundary actions—respecting Breuer’s self-reference bound by coarse-graining rather than exact self-copying.

Worked check 9.1. Which of the three pillars does a 2-qubit Bell pair fail, and which does a stateless feedforward image classifier fail?

Reasoned solution

A Bell pair has high quantum integration, yet fails thermodynamic autonomy and reflexive self-modeling. A feedforward image classifier processes external inputs without recurrent integration across layers or persistent autonomous self-modeling.

9.2 Formal definition of a conscious quantum subsystem

9.2.1 Coarse-grained pointer states, intrinsic integration, and reflexivity

Combining the mathematical machinery of Chapters 2–8, we state our formal definition:

Definition (Quantum-Compatible Consciousness). Within a composite quantum system \(\mathcal{H}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{H}_C \otimes \mathcal{H}_E\) evolving unitarily under \(U(t)\), a subsystem \(C\) realizes consciousness over an operational timescale \(\Delta t \gg \tau_D\) if there exists a decoherence-stabilized coarse-graining \(\Pi_C : \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_C) \to \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{H}_{\text{macro}})\) such that the induced open-system dynamics \(\mathcal{E}_{\Delta t}\) on \(\mathcal{H}_{\text{macro}}\) satisfies:

  1. Intrinsic Integration: \(\mathcal{E}_{\Delta t}\) has strictly positive, macroscopically significant integrated information \(\Phi(\rho_{\text{macro}}, \mathcal{E}_{\Delta t}) > 0\) across every partition of \(\mathcal{H}_{\text{macro}}\), binding sensory, memory, and evaluative degrees of freedom into one state;
  2. Autonomous Persistence: \(C\) sustains low-entropy pointer states and boundary integrity via continuous free-energy dissipation; and
  3. Reflexive Self-Model: A subalgebra of \(\mathcal{H}_{\text{macro}}\) represents a compressed, continuously updated predictive model of \((E, C)\)—so that \(C\)’s own internal transitions appear inside its model as the perspective of an acting subject.

Notice the elegance of \(\Pi_C\): it works whether \(\mathcal{H}_{\text{macro}}\) is an effectively classical pointer algebra (decohered neural firing patterns) or an actively error-corrected logical quantum subspace (a future fault-tolerant quantum agent).

Worked check 9.2. Why is the coarse-graining map \(\Pi_C\) essential in the definition above?

Reasoned solution

At the bare microscopic scale of individual thermalized atoms, states fluctuate and decohere in femtoseconds. Consciousness depends on the stable macro/mesoscopic information-bearing degrees of freedom selected by \(\Pi_C\) that persist long enough to integrate and model the world.

9.3 Compatibility check: unitarity, decoherence, and no-signalling

9.3.1 Verifying every postulate of quantum theory remains intact

Let us audit our definition against all four ground rules from Chapter 1:

  • Unitarity: Does consciousness alter \(U(t) = e^{-iHt/\hbar}\) on \(\mathcal{H}_C \otimes \mathcal{H}_E\)? No. All dynamics on \(\mathcal{H}_{\text{macro}}\) arise via standard partial trace and coarse-graining of unitary open-system evolution.
  • Decoherence and thermodynamics: Does it demand impossible room-temperature macroscopic superpositions? No. Stable pointer states and Landauer-bounded memory updates are built directly into the definition.
  • Relativistic no-signalling: Does a conscious subsystem transmit information faster than light? No. Any interaction across \(\mathcal{H}_C \otimes \mathcal{H}_E\) obeys local Hamiltonians and standard trace-preserving CPTP maps, so \(\operatorname{Tr}_C(\rho_{CE})\) cannot be steered remotely.
  • Non-circularity: Does it define “observer” using an unexplained “mind”? No. Every ingredient—tensor cuts, CPTP maps, \(\Phi\), entropy export, and predictive mutual information—is defined in physical and information-theoretic terms.

Worked check 9.3. If a conscious subsystem \(C\) observes one qubit of an entangled pair \(\ket{\Phi^+}_{AB}\), why does our definition guarantee that \(C\) cannot signal faster than light to Bob?

Reasoned solution

Because \(C\)’s observation is modeled as a local CPTP interaction on \(C \otimes A\) inside standard unitary quantum mechanics, Bob’s reduced state \(\rho_B = \operatorname{Tr}_{CA}(\rho_{CAB})\) remains strictly invariant.
